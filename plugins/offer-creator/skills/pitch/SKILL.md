# Pitch prep

Read `~/.lockedindept/offer/offer.md` and `~/.lockedindept/offer/pitch.md`. If either is missing, stop and tell the member to run `/offer-creator:offer` first. An offer this agent hasn't seen is a guess.

Ask for what they know about the client: who they are, what they said they want, where they came from, anything from their website or Instagram. Read whatever they give you.

Then produce one page, in this order:

1. **What they'll say they want** (surface) and **what's probably going on** (the real problem, from the offer's stage 3), with a note on how confident you are and what would confirm it.
2. **Five questions for this call**, adapted from the pitch script to this client. Ordered so the cost of the problem comes out of the client's own mouth before any price is mentioned.
3. **The likely prescription**: which tier, why it's the obvious one for this client, and the sentence that connects it to what they'll have just said.
4. **The price, said plainly.** The exact words, then "and then stop talking".
5. **What to listen for**: the two or three signals that mean go higher, and the one that means walk away.
6. **The two objections most likely from this client** and the honest reply.

Rules: their words and the offer's words, not generic sales language. No scripts that sound like a script. Never quote hours or a day rate. If the client is a poor fit for the avatar, say so plainly and suggest how to refer them on. British English, no em dashes.

After the call, if they tell you how it went, append three lines to `~/.lockedindept/offer/log.md`: date, client, what was quoted, outcome, one thing learned. That log is what makes the next prep sharper.
